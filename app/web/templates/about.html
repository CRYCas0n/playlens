How this works

Where the data comes from

Scores, descriptions and review texts come from Metacritic. We are an independent project and are not affiliated with Metacritic, with Fandom, or with any platform holder. Every page links back to the original, and we recommend reading it: we interpret the source, we do not replace it.

We currently hold {{ stats.games_total }} games, {{ stats.games_with_metascore }} of them with a critic score, and {{ stats.games_with_summaries }} with at least one summary. Discovery targets games that have a Metascore: without critic reviews there is no verdict to give and nothing to summarise.

What the numbers mean

Critic scores are published on a 0–100 scale and player scores on 0–10. We display each in its own scale and compare them on the 0–100 axis, multiplying the player score by ten. Bands: 85+ Excellent, 70–84 Good, 50–69 Mixed, below 50 Poor.

A missing score is shown as “Not rated”, never as zero. The source sometimes reports a player score of 0 for a game with one or two ratings and no written reviews; treating that as an opinion would be false, so we do not.

The verdict sentence

The sentence at the top of every game page is calculated, not written. It is composed from the two scores, so it can never contradict the numbers beside it and carries no AI label. The same is true of the line comparing platforms.

The summaries

The summaries are written by a language model, from the reviews themselves and from nothing else. Each point lists how many reviews support it, and a point that cannot cite at least three independent reviews is discarded before publication. So is a claim about change over time whose cited reviews do not span enough dates.

If a game has no criticism in its reviews, the “most criticised” list is empty and we say so. We would rather show an empty section than invent a complaint to fill it. Where a game has too few written reviews to summarise honestly, no summary appears.

Player reviews are filtered before summarising: duplicates, very short posts, reviews whose text plainly contradicts their own score, and reviews about matters other than the game. Every summary states how many reviews it used out of how many we hold.

Similar games

Suggestions are drawn only from games already in our index, so every one of them opens a page like this. They are scored on genre, series, studio, platforms, era, score proximity and the aspects reviewers praised. Where we can name the reason we show it; where we cannot, we show no reason rather than inventing one.

What we do not do

We do not publish a score of our own. Our job is to explain other people's numbers, not to add another one to the pile.
